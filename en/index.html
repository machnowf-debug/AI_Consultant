01 Situation

The pilot worked.
Production did not.

In almost every marketing team I speak to, AI has already arrived: as a licence, as a trial, as a presentation. What is missing is the step from tool to process. That is exactly where the savings live, and exactly where most initiatives stall.

Where most marketing teams stand today
  • AI licences are bought but barely visible in day-to-day work.
  • Output has not noticeably grown, agency invoices have not shrunk.
  • Every discipline experiments on its own. None of it is a documented process.
  • Uncertainty around GDPR, the EU AI Act and image rights blocks sign-off.
  • Nobody can quantify what AI has delivered to marketing so far.
Where you stand after the change
  • A defined production process instead of isolated attempts.
  • Creative, editorial, web and social work in one shared pipeline.
  • Cost per asset and cycle time are measured, before and after.
  • Quality control, sign-off and documentation are settled.
  • Your team keeps it running once I am gone.

07 Frequent questions

What you are probably asking

You show no client logos. Why not?

Because I would rather stay honest. I recently went independent. My results come from in-house accountability, not from consulting mandates, and the companies involved have not granted me promotional clearance. A wall of logos would simply be a lie.

What I do instead: I disclose companies, timeframes, my role and the source of every number in the intro call and answer any follow-up question. Anyone who wants to verify gets contacts from my professional network.

What does it cost?

I work with fixed fees per stage, not open day rates. I name the order of magnitude openly in the intro call, as soon as I know the size of your team and how many disciplines are affected.

What I will not do is quote a fee before I know the scope. That would be a number without content.

We already have ChatGPT Enterprise or Copilot. Is that not enough?

A licence is not a process. In nearly every team I see, a few individuals use the tools well and everyone else does not. Departmental output does not change because of that.

The jump only happens once work steps are recut, quality loops defined and approvals settled. That is what I work on, using the tools you already own.

Do you replace our agency?

No. I assess which work is faster and cheaper in-house and which is better placed outside. Usually the agency stays but shifts away from production towards concept and peak craft.

I have steered five agencies and know how both sides do the maths.

How do we stay compliant with GDPR and the EU AI Act?

By planning for it from the start instead of repairing afterwards. I prefer models that can be run in Europe, keep personal data out of the workflows and document which model sees which data at which point.

I am not a lawyer and I do not give legal advice. I deliver the structure and documentation your legal team or data protection officer can work with.

How much internal time does this take?

Around six hours across your team during the audit, mostly interviews and data access. During the implementation sprint the people involved work on their real tasks, simply in a different way. I deliberately keep additional workshops short.

Does the knowledge stay with us after you leave?

That is the point of the exercise. Processes, prompts, standards and operating instructions belong to you and end up in your systems. A mandate that creates dependency would be a bad deal for you and a poor business model for me.

Who are you not right for?

Anyone looking for a keynote, anyone wanting to buy finished software, and anyone expecting AI to solve a staffing problem in four weeks. I am also a poor fit when nobody inside the organisation wants to own the transition. Without an internal owner, every project stalls, including mine.
